A model answers by generating the tokens that are likely to follow the context, one at a time. Training made text that resembles its training data likely, and true statements are common in that data, so the likely continuation is often the true one. But nothing in generation checks it. When the model has not absorbed the fact, the likely continuation is still something that looks like an answer: a plausible name, a reasonable-sounding date, a citation in the right format to a paper that does not exist.

There is no lookup to fail. What a model knows is spread across its weights, not kept as records, so there is no row that comes back empty and no “not found” to report. Saying “I don’t know” is itself just another continuation, and it is only chosen when training made it the likely one in that kind of context.

Nor is it lying. Models do carry some internal signal of how uncertain they are, and training can teach them to act on it, but the signal is imperfect, and a fluent wrong answer is produced by the same process as a fluent right one. Errors are most common for things that appeared rarely in training: obscure people, exact numbers, quotations, recent events.

The fixes all work by putting a check outside the model’s memory:

  • Grounding: retrieve the relevant text and answer from it, which helps exactly as much as retrieval finds the right passage;
  • Tools: look it up, calculate it or run it rather than recall it;
  • Verification: check the answer, by tests, by citations that can be opened, or by sampling several times and distrusting answers that disagree;
  • Training that rewards declining to answer over guessing.
